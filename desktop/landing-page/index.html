What it does

Every clip, sorted into three piles.

Mimir looks at motion, objects, and where they are relative to your car, then puts each event where it belongs. You start at the top and stop when it gets boring.

How it works

Four steps, then you are reviewing.

  1. 01

    Copy the footage across

    Drag TeslaCam off the USB stick onto your drive. Scanning straight from the stick is slow, and hard on the drive.

  2. 02

    Point Mimir at the folder

    It tells you what hardware it found and roughly how long this will take before you commit to anything.

  3. 03

    Let it scan

    About 25 minutes for a full 679-clip dump on a machine with a graphics card. Go and do something else.

  4. 04

    Review what surfaced

    Each clip has a timeline marking where Mimir thinks something happened, so you jump straight there instead of watching the whole minute.

Privacy

Your footage never leaves your PC.

Not as a promise about how we handle uploads — as a description of what the software does. Scanning happens entirely on your machine. There is no account, no cloud, no telemetry, and no analytics.

Two things can leave

Written feedback about a verdict, and — separately — a clip you explicitly choose to contribute. Both only when you press a button.

Encrypted before it moves

Anything you do send is encrypted on your own machine first, with a key held by the intake, not by the network in between.

Nothing is automatic

No background sync, no opt-out buried in settings. If you never press the button, nothing is ever sent.

Try it on a week of your own footage.

It is free, it runs offline, and the worst case is that you close it again.

Requirements and things worth knowing first
Operating system
Windows 10 or 11, 64-bit. No macOS or Linux build yet.
Graphics
Optional, but it matters. With a DirectX 12 GPU expect about 25 minutes for a full dump; on the processor alone, hours. Mimir tells you which you have before you start.
Disk
About 325 MB installed, plus room for the footage you copy over.
Internet
Only to fetch Microsoft’s WebView2 at install time if your PC lacks it, and if you choose to send feedback. Scanning and review are fully offline.

Windows will warn you on first run

The installer is not code-signed yet, so SmartScreen shows a blue “Windows protected your PC” box. Click More info, then Run anyway. Windows is reporting that it does not know who made this — not that the file is dangerous. If that is not good enough, the source builds are right there.

It is early beta, and it over-flags

Treat Important as “look at this”, not “something happened”. Mimir rules out well over half of a week’s events and puts the rest in front of you, which beats watching all of them but is not the ten-out-of-seven-hundred it is aiming for. The known issues page has the real numbers and what is being done about them.
