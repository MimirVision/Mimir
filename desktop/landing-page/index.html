How it works

Four steps, then you are reviewing.

  1. 01

    Copy the footage across

    Drag TeslaCam off the USB stick onto your drive. Scanning straight from the stick is slow, and hard on the drive.

  2. 02

    Point Mimir at the folder

    It tells you what hardware it found and roughly how long this will take before you commit to anything.

  3. 03

    Let it scan

    About 25 minutes for a full 679-clip dump on a machine with a graphics card. Go and do something else.

  4. 04

    Review what surfaced

    Each clip has a timeline marking where Mimir thinks something happened, so you jump straight there instead of watching the whole minute.

Where it stands

It over-flags. Here are the numbers.

Of the beta feedback so far, 18 of 19 clips Mimir called Important were rated lower by the person who actually watched them. That is not a measured error rate — people write in when something is wrong, not when it is right — but the direction is not in doubt.

Two rules were tightened in August 2026 after reading that feedback back. Replayed against it, the change removes 17 of the 18 Important calls a human rejected, and keeps the one they agreed with.

But those clips dropped to Review rather than disappearing, and Review is where your evening actually goes. On a real 656-event dump Mimir now rules out 378 and asks you to look at 278. Better than watching all 656. Nowhere near good enough.

Almost all of those 278 are flagged because something was near the car and something moved — which, for a parked car, is most of the time. Separating that from actual contact is the problem being worked on, and it needs a properly labelled set of footage to measure against rather than more guessing.

Telling Mimir when it is wrong is the single most useful thing a tester can do. The detector cannot improve without examples, and a clip it misjudged is worth more than a hundred it got right.

  • It cannot prove contact happened. Two shapes overlapping is not evidence of touching.
  • It does not identify people or read number plates. Deliberately, permanently.
  • It never deletes or moves anything on its own.

Privacy

Your footage never leaves your PC.

Not as a promise about how we handle uploads — as a description of what the software does. Scanning happens entirely on your machine. There is no account, no cloud, no telemetry, and no analytics.

Two things can leave

Written feedback about a verdict, and — separately — a clip you explicitly choose to contribute. Both only when you press a button.

Encrypted before it moves

Anything you do send is encrypted on your own machine first, with a key held by the intake, not by the network in between.

Nothing is automatic

No background sync, no opt-out buried in settings. If you never press the button, nothing is ever sent.

Before you download

What it needs, and what it will do to you.

Operating system
Windows 10 or 11, 64-bit. No macOS or Linux build.
Graphics
Optional, but it matters. Object detection is over half the scan time and runs roughly 10× slower on the processor alone. With a DirectX 12 GPU, expect about 25 minutes for a full dump; without one, budget hours. Mimir tells you which you have before you start.
Disk
About 325 MB installed, plus room for the footage you copy over.
Internet
Only if Windows is missing the WebView2 runtime at install time, and if you choose to send feedback. Scanning and review are fully offline.
Prerequisites
None. No Python, no runtimes to install, no account to create.

Windows will warn you on first run

The installer is not code-signed yet, so SmartScreen shows a blue “Windows protected your PC” box. Click More info, then Run anyway.

That warning is accurate and worth understanding rather than clicking past. Windows checks whether an installer was signed by a verified publisher. Mimir is not signed, because a certificate costs several hundred dollars a year and this is free. So Windows correctly reports that it does not know who made this — not that the file is dangerous. If that is not good enough, the source builds are right there.

Try it on a week of your own footage.
